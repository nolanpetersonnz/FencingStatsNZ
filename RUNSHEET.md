## Standing rules (keep in view)

- Mid-task, say only: "What are you thinking?" / "What did you expect there?"
- Never name a page, explain a feature, or point at the screen during a task.
- Stuck 90 seconds, or asks to move on: say "I'm going to give you a hint now", give the task's hint, tick the hint box, continue. Second hint only where listed. Otherwise skip.
- Criticism gets "say more about that". Nothing else, ever.
- Data looks wrong: note it, don't debug.
- SEQ after every task, before any probe, same words every time: "Overall, how easy or hard was that? 1 is very difficult, 7 is very easy."
- Probes come after SEQ. If you paraphrase a prompt and they don't get it, re-read it as written.

## 0. Opening (read word for word, after pressing record)

"Thanks for doing this. Quick recap of what you agreed to: this is a 60-minute session to test the fencing stats site. I've just started recording screen and audio. The recording stays private with me, except that one other person may check a couple of recordings for scoring, and it's deleted within six months of the findings going up. Transcripts are made with automated transcription software, so the audio is processed by that service, and I check every transcript myself. You can skip anything, stop at any time, or tell me afterwards to delete all of it, right up until the findings are published on the project's GitHub. The anonymised findings might also feed into future academic work. Two things to remember. First, I'm testing the site, not you. If something's confusing or broken, it's the site's fault, not yours. Finding issues is the whole reason I'm doing this. Second, I built the site, but you can't hurt my feelings. Critical feedback is really important, and being polite to me would actually waste both of our time. Please think out loud as you go: what you're looking at, what you're expecting, what surprises you. Happy to go ahead with the recording on?"
