# Can fencers read their own ratings? Part 2: findings

Part 1 is the study protocol ([PDF](Can%20Fencer's%20Read%20Their%20Own%20Ratings%20-%20A%20User%20Study.pdf)), preregistered on OSF on 2026-09-28 ([registration](https://osf.io/ns3zj/overview)) before the first main-sample session. The coding rules are in [CODEBOOK.md](CODEBOOK.md), version 1.5. Every change made after registration is listed in section 2.2.

## Summary

Yes, fencers can read their own ratings. However, they can only do so with help or a nudge. By the end of the session, once they have seen the site, nearly everyone understands that the rating is a relative measure of their skill as a fencer, which is derived from their wins and losses. However, they don't understand the exact mechanism or how the headline is displayed before being shown. What matters most is that they're trusting the numbers the right amount, knowing that while the number is accurate to an extent, it is not a hill to die on.

## How this report was made

- Data: eight moderated remote think-aloud sessions (section 2.1), coded in a private workbook: 551 observations, 41 issues.
- Every claim cites the observation ids (Oxxx) it rests on. Quotes are verbatim from transcripts checked against the recordings, with other fencers' names removed.
- Reporting rules (codebook section 9): participants by code only; no percentages; evidence against a finding gets the same space as evidence for it; T5 wager answers as counts only; SUS in aggregate only.

The first-pass coding was drafted with AI from automatic transcripts (Zoom's for P07 and P08, an offline speech-to-text tool for P01 to P06). It also compiled the tables and lists of evidence from the workbook. Every code was hand-checked against the recordings, and findings, interpretation, and design decisions were made by me.

## 2.5 Findings by research question

### RQ1. Do fencers understand that the shown number is the low end of the uncertainty, and that it rises with more data, not just wins?

My interpretation is that this is something that's not obvious. I would argue that people are not describing the number as the low end of a range because they don't see it as relevant. My intuition is that people understand it's the low end of a range, but they still see the headline number as the number, not the rating as a range. Two participants are exceptions: P03 read the range as running "from the pool to the DE" and said "I'm not understanding" (O178, O180), and P08 asked "Uncertainty is this number?" late in the session (O570). This is something that would benefit from further clarification. How inactivity is handled should also be clearly communicated; the decay should be replaced with a one-year window, because that is the selection period (2.9).

How the live site behaves: the headline is the low end of the uncertainty range; the range narrows as bouts accumulate, so the headline rises with more data, not only with wins. The inactivity decay is off, so not fencing changes nothing.

Confirming evidence

- No low end or range in the unprompted account, all eight (ABS-LOWEND, ABS-RANGE): P01 O052, O053; P02 O110, O111; P03 O180, O181; P04 O238, O239; P05 O308, O309; P06 O374, O375; P07 O445, O446; P08 O512, O513.
  - P02, an existing user: "Well I don't know what it's saying about me... my peak? I dunno" (O110). At the debrief: "I still really don't know. Is that helpful?" (O159).
  - P07: "I'm assuming that these are my ratings... I'm assuming the higher the better" (O445).
  - P08: "It didn't say anything about me as a fencer. It gives some numerical number" (O512).

### RQ2. Can fencers decipher the pool and DE ratings and connect them to a training decision?

Yes, they can. This is a feature that is not relevant to every fencer, but the ones with a big gap are able to see this. However, it is very rarely a surprise to a fencer that they are stronger in the pool or in the DE. The connection is easy to make when the gap is big. However, the training decision should only be there when the gap is big. So if they don't identify that there is something to change between their pools and DEs, it's probably because it's not there.

Confirming evidence

- Training choice grounded in the two rating boxes:
  - P02: "replicate the conditions more of direct elimination... I perform worse in the direct eliminations than pools... these numbers" (O114).
  - P05: "if you're going off the raw numbers here... just the main number" (O315); the moderator completed the inference.
  - P06: "I'll be focusing on DEs... the DE is a lower number, so you'd want to work on trying to improve that" (O380).
  - P07: "I would spend that extra training on working on my pool... as it is lower than my DE rating" (O451).
- Grounded in the rating chart's two lines: P01, "probably direct elimination, according to the trend line" (O056), before the hint that capped the task.
- Split recognised at once: P04 "my pools and my elimination, so that's separate" (O240); P05 (O307); P06 "I'm now working out that's a pool and that's a DE" (O372); P07 "the first thing I'm seeing is my rating in pools and DEs" (O444); P08 "Apparently, the pool actually performed better than DE. That's useful to know" (O514), and compared his split with the field's on the ladder (O526).
- The split as a reason to trust the numbers more: P02 (O130), P03 (O223), P04 (O281).
- The page confirmed what P02 (O115) and P05 (O317) already believed, and showed P07 something new about his win rates (O454).

Disconfirming evidence

- P03 could not read the split and used the worst-matchups table instead: "I would assume this tells me something, but because I don't understand it, I wouldn't be able to tell" (O186); "these two people are both lefties... I'm lacking with lefties" (O184). The pilot participant did the same. I-17.
- P08 read the split correctly ("The pool result is slightly better than the DE", O520) and made no training choice: "It's not entirely clear to me" (O518). T2 Fail. He looked at the matchups but did not know where they come from (O521; I-41).
- P06 treated the two scales as directly comparable ("you would imagine are equivalent", O380). The page does not say whether they are.
- P07 read the win-rate line faster than the rating boxes: "it's simplified here with a percentage, and so I was like, I can see that immediately" (O452).
- P01 used the chart, not the boxes.

Not tested

- P04 has no lost bouts in the dataset, so no weaker stream and no worst matchups: "the site doesn't really tell me anything in terms of what I need to be training" (O249). T2 n/a.

### RQ3. Does the default "All ages" leaderboard mislead people about where they stand for selection?

No, it does not. "All ages" should be rephrased to "Open" or "Senior". Otherwise, there is no issue. The real issue is in the veteran category banding, and Vet 40, Vet 50, Vet 60 and Vet 70 categories should be included. This would be a feature that the current official Fencing New Zealand site does not have.

Confirming evidence (the age view misleads or falls short)

- P06 (veteran):
  - One veteran category stands for four real age bands: "so it doesn't make it very easy for me to see who I would be able to rank up against" (O383).
  - The veterans filter captures one age band, so he and most other veterans were not shown: "interestingly enough, I'm not even in the veterans" (O384); "there's far more veterans in New Zealand than that" (O385). T3 failed on this defect (I-13).

## 2.9 Notes for DESIGN.md (protocol section 10)

Each DESIGN.md decision the study bears on, what the study shows, and what you will change.

1. "Showing uncertainty: a range, not just a number". Nobody read the headline as a low end unprompted; four built their own model of the number (RQ1, I-01).
   *Decision:* Keep the conservative headline. If not, you get fencers that skyrocket to the top with a very small data set. However, it should be made more clear, including on the ledger. On that note, "Ledger" should be renamed to "Leaderboard".
2. "Decaying old results". The decay is shipped but off; readers expected inactive fencers to drop, and recency was the most common reason to discount a prediction (RQ1, RQ5, I-10, I-11).
   *Decision:* Decay does not fit the nature of fencing. It should be replaced so that only one year's worth of results are counted at a time. However, we should extend the dataset to contain all historical bouts. That way, head-to-head is extended, and people can see their historical matchups better. However, it should not count towards the rating. A one-year window is the right period because that is the selection period.
3. "Club strength tiers" and the "where should I fence?" paragraph. Six of seven looked for region first; strength was read against club size (RQ6, I-02, I-26).
   *Decision:* Club strength tiers, as said before, are not relevant. If "where should I fence?" is the main question, we should include a map and a region sort.
4. The calibration backtest, surfaced publicly. The headline number was read; Brier, log loss and the calibration section mostly were not; the page does not say the backtest is out of sample (RQ5, I-06, I-07, I-40).
   *Decision:* Brier and log loss were not read because they don't make sense to a layperson; the calibration chart was read correctly by P01, P04 and P08. The page also does not say the backtest is out of sample.
5. The DE tableau line grading. The table was found unaided by one of eight (RQ4, I-03, I-19, I-20, I-39). DESIGN.md still describes the pre-June line ("who had the best chance of sweeping their draw"); since 2026-06-05 the site measures the whole path to the title.
   *Decision:* The sweep odds need an updated description, because if you sweep the competition by winning all of your line, you win. That's what it means. I feel people struggled to find the tableau due to the nature of the question. If they were looking for the tableau, they would find it. However, going from the tableau page and scrolling down to find sweep odds may be difficult.
6. "RD is a concept fencers don't intuitively understand... I've added a note in the FAQ". Nobody consulted the FAQ for the rating; four asked for an explanation on the page (I-01).
   *Decision:* I feel that people don't look at the FAQ because they were in a test, an interview with me, and they are not going to stop for the FAQ mid-session. But also, it could be humanized a bit more and made more concise.

## 2.11 Limitations

Registered (protocol section 11), and what happened:

- Single coder (codebook 1.4(h)): every score rests on one coder who is also the moderator and the developer, working from AI-drafted first-pass codes checked against the recordings (1.1(e)). No agreement figure.
- The moderator is the developer: 43 explanations, 31 leading prompts, 12 hints, six of them early (section 2.2). Participants blamed themselves: P01 "that's probably on me" (O087); P03 "I'm not good with the data" (O220).
- The T7 before/after question pushes for a change. P06's after-number was arithmetic on the accuracy figure (O429); P04 first read the after-question as rating the page's effect (O286).
- n = 8 finds understanding failures and usability issues, not frequency or preference.
- 18+ only, no cadets. One junior and two veterans, so RQ3 rests on three people.
- Recruited from a community that knew of the project. P01, P07 and P08 were the only fresh users (P03 had not seen the site but knew of it). One participant is a research collaborator of the moderator on a separate project; they are not identified here.
- Fixed task order.
- First use only, one session.

Observed in addition:

- Sign-in discoverability untested in all eight sessions (instructed).
- SUS aggregate only, on a 7-point form with agreement at 1. SEQ not asked for P01 and never after T7.
- Ease ratings did not track success: everyone asked rated T1 and T5 a 7, while four of seven T1s and five of seven T5s were Partial (section 2.3).
- Two comprehension golds described something other than the live site until they were corrected: C2 scored the site's own sweep-odds definition as a misreading (1.4(b)); C1 assumed the inactivity decay was on (1.5(a)). No score changed; one issue (I-18) and two codes (O405, O517) had been built on them.
- Wager answers could be matched to participants inside the private workbook until 2026-10-30 (1.3(f), 1.4(g)).
- P08's session was also captured by a third-party AI notetaker, beyond what the consent text describes (1.4(d)).
- No selector-oriented task, although a selector was recruited.
- Transcripts automatic (P01 to P06) or Zoom's (P07, P08), checked against the recordings; speaker attribution errors are possible where none was flagged.
- P01 ran on a stand-in profile, so her RQ1 and RQ4 evidence is about reading someone else's numbers.
- P04's T2 could not run (no losses), so RQ2 rests on seven.
- The 48-hour coding limit was missed for P04 and P05.
- In T4 the moderator named a competition P02 was not in, and P03 opened the wrong championships twice because of similar names. Both inflate T4 time; only the P03 case counts for I-22.

If I were the moderator again, I would first avoid leading the participant and giving hints that weren't needed. I found it difficult to let people struggle, and I felt a need to push people to get task completion. I had also said that it's important that people are critical of me. However, with P08, I became defensive at times, which should not happen. If I were to redesign the study, I would use less structure, in order to probe deeper when I saw fit.
